## Control premium vs AI premium (concept)
Control premium = price over the unaffected trading value. AI valuation premium = difference in acquisition multiple versus comparable non-AI targets. They are not synonyms: a 20x EV/Revenue deal can carry a 10% bid premium if the stock already traded richly; a 5x business can carry a 50% premium.

## R/S/T/X-AB. Frameworks (HYPOTHESES - not empirically identified)
- Strategic-value sources: revenue synergy, cost synergy, time-to-market, defensive value, data/model value, talent (operating company vs excluded acqui-hires).
- Build vs buy: a strategic may rationally pay if PV(time saved + synergies + option value) > acquisition premium.
- Scarcity premium = valuation attributed to rare strategic assets with limited substitutes; no numeric estimate is made.
- Growth vs scarcity: growth premium = higher multiple from faster growth; residual after controls is the only candidate AI-specific component, and it also contains omitted variables.

- Precedent decision tree: is the target AI-native (intensity >= 2)? -> is the effect robust (Phase 6 label)? YES -> prioritise AI-native precedents; NO -> match on growth, scale, margin, regime, buyer type. Never add a mechanical % uplift.
- Banker workflow: sector precedents -> AI intensity -> normalise growth/margin -> separate public/private -> timing -> buyer mix -> conditional AI effect -> judgement.

## AD. Deal-team takeaways
1. Do not compare AI targets on EV/Revenue alone.
2. Growth, margin and size controls matter materially (see Gelbach).
3. Do not pool AI-enabled (level 1) with AI-native (levels 2-3) automatically.
4. Transaction multiple and takeover premium are different objects.
5. Public-market valuation may already embed AI expectations (see case A-D).
6. Strategic and sponsor buyers may value AI differently (see interaction).
7. Match precedents on sector and year.
8. Extreme early-stage revenue multiples need caution.
9. Private-target disclosure is selective.
10. Choose precedents for economic comparability, not labels.
11. Treat any residual AI effect as context, not a standalone adjustment.
12. Quantity of AI deals and price of AI deals are separate questions.

## AE. Interview insights
**What did you find?** No empirical conclusion can be drawn: the sample does not meet the pre-specified power gates.
**Did AI cause the premium?** No causal claim; association conditional on observables, with selection into being acquired.
**Why EV/Revenue?** Many targets have negative EBITDA; revenue is the common denominator.
**Why not EV/EBITDA?** Undefined or extreme for loss-making targets; kept as a secondary outcome.
**Why log the multiple?** Right-skewed; log differences read as percentage gaps.
**Why HC3?** Small samples with high-leverage points; HC3 is conservative.
**Why matching?** To compare AI deals only with similar non-AI deals and test the regression's functional-form dependence.
**Why bootstrap?** Inference without relying on normal errors in small samples.
**Why placebo?** Randomly reassigning AI labels shows how large a coefficient arises by chance.
**What explains the raw gap?** See the Gelbach table: the share attributable to growth, margin, size, year, sector, region, buyer/public.
**Why might strategics pay more?** HYPOTHESES: synergies, time-to-market, defensive value, IP and talent; none identified here.
**Why might takeover premium not differ?** Public stocks may already price AI expectations; premium is measured against unaffected value.
**How would a banker use it?** No change to precedent practice can be inferred from this project yet.
**Biggest limitation?** No causal identification; selection into being acquired; private disclosure bias; treatment error.
**What with Capital IQ?** Systematic universe, more deals, pre-deal multiples, contested-process data, then repeat the frozen pipeline.
**What was excluded?** Acqui-hires, non-technology sectors, deals without disclosed EV/revenue; see the waterfall.
